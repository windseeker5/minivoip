How a "Call me" tap reaches your phone

Four places are involved: the guest's phone, your computer at home, Google's (or Apple's) notification service, and your phone. Step through a call below to see what moves where.

Why two containers

One connects the call, the other carries the voice

Container 1 · callbox

The switchboard

  • Serves the call screen and your Appels app
  • Notices when someone presses 0
  • Asks Google or Apple to make your phone ring
  • Passes the short setup notes between the two phones
  • Keeps voicemails

It never touches the voice. It's the receptionist who connects you and hangs up.

Container 2 · coturn

The voice relay

  • A meeting point both phones can always reach
  • Used only when the phones can't talk directly, which is common on cellular
  • Needs its own doors in the router: 3478, plus 49160–49200 for the voice itself

Caddy can't do this job. It only handles web pages; voice is a constant stream of small packets.

Why phones often can't reach each other directly

Your home router and the cellular carriers each act like a front desk that only lets in replies to calls made from the inside. Two phones behind two front desks can't make the first call to each other. So both call out to coturn, which has an open door, and coturn passes the voice across. When the networks are friendly, the phones find each other directly and coturn stays idle.

And Asterisk?

Asterisk is only needed for a real phone number

Asterisk is a free phone-system program. It's what you'd run if people dialed a regular 10-digit number: the phone company hands the call to Asterisk, Asterisk plays the menu and transfers to someone. It came up in the research, and the homemade plan dropped it on purpose.

In the homemade version there is no phone number. The call starts on a web page, so the guest's own browser plays the menu, and Asterisk would have nothing to do. Zadarma is the same idea run by someone else: they host the phone system and rent you the number.

The router doors

Three doors, one still closed

443

Web pages and setup notes, through Caddy. Same door your site already uses.

Open
3478

"Hello coturn, I need a relay." UDP and TCP.

To open
49160–49200

The voice itself, about four doors per call. UDP.

To open

Both new doors need a forward on the router to this computer, and a rule in this computer's firewall. Until then the call connects and then stays silent on cellular. That's exactly what the relay test showed.
